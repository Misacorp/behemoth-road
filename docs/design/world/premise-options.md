# Why behemoths travel: premise options

A proposal for Misa, 24 November 2026. It answers your notes on world v3:

- You want creatures to become part of the world, but not every kind can become a hill.
- You don't want an in-world "road".
- You asked who the party are and who sends them.

Every name and line here is a sample.

**What each premise has to do:**

- Work for many kinds: the moss grazer, magma serpent, sky-whale or storm bird, reef creature, frost beast and forest giant.
- Allow journeys of several legs, and changes of direction.
- Leave the peoples room to disagree. The narrator states one visible fact, and the legends argue about the rest.
- Leave something behind when a creature comes home, for the sanctuary.

## 1. Four premises

### A. The waking places

**Pitch.** Each kind belongs to one kind of place: a fold, a fire mountain, a reef, a glacier, a wood. A place that no creature of its kind has visited in a long time falls asleep. A behemoth goes to a sleeping place of its kind and settles there for good, and the place wakes around it. The peoples argue over whether the creature is dead, asleep, or has simply become the place.

**Endings:**

- **Mossback:** It walks into the bare fold and does not come out. By the Waking the fold is green from rim to rim.
- **Magma serpent:** It coils down into the cold crater. Three nights later the mountain smokes, and the snow on its shoulders is gone.
- **Reef creature:** It sinks onto the white reef. By the next low tide the reef has colour again, and fish in it.
- **Forest giant:** It stands in the burnt valley and stays. In spring, saplings come up in a ring around it.

**Regions and events.** The land around a sleeping place is poor: ash, white coral, bare hills. The map grows barer as you get closer, then wakes behind you. Events come from people who have built their lives around the sleep. The Wheelhouse has mills on the dry riverbed and does not want the river back.

**Sanctuary.** Each creature becomes a woken place, kept on a chart. On later maps it shows up as a new region, such as ash fields or a living reef.

**Risks.** Every good ending is the end of the creature, so it reads as a sacrifice with the players as its couriers. Each kind also repeats one move with new scenery. And the hill folk's "sleeper" becomes almost true, which breaks the rule that no legend is ever confirmed.

### B. The carriers

**Pitch.** Something grows on each behemoth as it travels: seed in the Mossback's moss, embers in the serpent's belly, rain in the sky-whale's hide, spawn in the reef creature's shell. It must put this load down where the land lacks it, sometimes in two or three places, and then it goes back to the wild. The narrator states that things grow on it and that the land changes where it puts them down. The peoples argue about what the load is (seed, luck, the dead's dreams, dung) and who it is for.

**Endings:**

- **Mossback:** It shakes its moss out over the bare slope and walks off lighter. By summer the slope is a meadow, with thistle and fern from three regions.
- **Magma serpent:** It spills its embers into the cold springs. Steam stands over the valley for a week, and the serpent is gone into the hills.
- **Sky-whale:** It lets its rain go over the cracked plain all night. In the morning it is a speck in the north, and the plain is mud.
- **Frost beast:** It leaves its cold on the bare pass. Snow lies there through midsummer.

**Regions and events.** Regions are defined by what they lack. Multi-leg journeys come naturally: one load, set down in several places. Events are peoples trying to get the load set down on their own land. The Reedchoir, singing it into the fen, fits as written.

**Sanctuary.** The places where a load was set down. The creature is still alive and may turn up again on a later map.

**Risks.** It plays like a delivery job, with the creature as a cart, which is exactly how the Wheelhouse sees it. Something has to decide where the land lacks a load, so the map needs a planner behind it. And the garden on the creature's back stops being flavour and becomes cargo, a mechanic.

### C. The rounds and the hearths

**Pitch.** Each kind walks a great circle forever between hearths of its element: a ring of stones on a hill, a fire crater, a still place in the high air, a ring reef, an ice seat. A hearth that nobody visits goes cold, and its land sickens. The party take a creature on one leg of its round, or several, from the pen to the next hearth it missed. There it kindles the hearth, rests through the Settling and then goes on alone. The peoples argue over who built the hearths and whether a round can be broken.

**Endings:**

- **Mossback:** It reaches the stone ring on the hill, and by morning moss covers the stones. In spring it goes on west, alone.
- **Magma serpent:** It coils into the cold crater, and the crater glows at night. By the Waking the glow is still there and the serpent is not.
- **Storm bird:** It settles on the bare peak. Thunder comes back to the valley for the first time in a lifetime.
- **Reef creature:** It circles the ring reef three times. The next day the ring is bright.

**Regions and events.** Maps are laid out around hearths. Old cold hearths are sites for the Scholar. A lure event is a false hearth, and the parked leaning fires would fit there.

**Sanctuary.** A chart of the hearths your group has lit. A lit hearth warms its region on later maps.

**Risks.**

- "The balance of the world" is a stock fantasy frame.
- Great circuits across the land come close to Aboriginal songlines, which the research warns against.
- Fixed hearths are a road in disguise.
- The creature walks out of the story at the end, so the sanctuary holds places, not creatures.

### D. The casting

**Pitch.** Every behemoth carries its years in an outer coat: moss, scale, shell, cloud, fleece or bark. When the coat grows heavy, the creature goes to a casting ground of its kind and casts the coat there. The coat stays and becomes land, and something small and young goes on into the fire, the sea, the sky or the snow. The narrator states only that much. The peoples argue about the rest: whether the young one is the same creature, what the coat keeps, whether casting is a kind of death, and what happens if a coat is broken open.

**The coat holds the journey.** v3's garden on the creature's back (2.2) is the coat filling up with the journey. Whatever grows on it along the way ends up in the land it leaves behind.

**Endings:**

- **Mossback:** In the Greenfold it turns three times and casts its moss. By morning there is a new hill with thistle, fern and white sand in its grass, and small grey tracks lead over the rise.
- **Magma serpent:** It casts its skin in the caldera. The skin cools into a ridge of black glass, and something bright slips down into the fire.
- **Sky-whale:** It casts its cloud over the dry valley. The cloud stays and rains there every evening, and a small shape swims up into the blue.
- **Reef creature:** It leaves its shell on the shoal. By the spring tide the shell is a reef, and small tracks lead down into deep water.
- **Frost beast:** It casts its winter coat in the high pass. The snow lies there all summer, and the streams below run all year.
- **Forest giant:** It casts its antlers in the clearing, and by the Waking they have taken root.
- **Wounded:** the ridge is cracked, or the hill a little crooked. **Lost (`hunger`):** it casts in the wrong place, and the ground there comes up pale (v3's fragment 7 already says this).

**Regions and events.**

- Any hill, ridge, reef or snowfield might be an old coat, so the land itself is the archive.
- Tombs become old coats that the Scholar opens.
- Multi-leg journeys follow from readying the coat first: the serpent must drink at a hot spring to loosen its skin, the reef creature must find a river mouth before the sea, and the frost beast must reach snow.
- The casting ground is any ground of its kind, found by following signs, so the destination can move.
- The Wheelhouse gets a fair motive: coats make flour, glass and salt. A penned creature casts a small coat in the pen, and the land gets nothing.

**Sanctuary.** It has two parts:

- **The coats** on a chart of the Long Country, each holding that season's garden.
- **The young** that went free, which may cross later maps as echoes (progression §3 already has echoes).

**Risks.**

- Casting is something the body does. Keep it to the endings, so it never competes with the Keeper's signals.
- An old self becoming land is close to Ymir and Pangu. Keep it small and local.
- Each kind needs its own art for its casting ground.

## 2. Who the party are, and who sends them

### Option 1: the Open Gate, an old company

The Open Gate is a company with no people of its own, bound by an old law: no one fences a walker. Its members come from every people. Each people tells a different story of how the Gate began. The hill folk say it was founded by the collar-makers' grandchildren, paying off a debt. The Wheelhouse calls them thieves. The Caravan says the Gate still owes it for something and won't say what.

- **The pick:** during the Settling, word reaches the Gate of two pens. The two veiled creatures are those two pens.
- **Recruits:** people met on the way who join the Gate.
- **Objective:** a vow made at the gate, chosen from two.
- _Two pens, and one company. The Gate can open only one before the Waking._
- _Haldo lifts the latch and stands back. He has done this before; you can tell from where he stands._

### Option 2: the peoples ask

Each Settling, two peoples bring a token for a creature they need set free. The party are whoever answers.

- **The pick:** the two tokens are the two veiled creatures, for example a pearl and a scrap of black glass.
- **Recruits:** they come from the people whose token you took up.
- **Objective:** something that people asked of you, chosen from two.
- _The river folk set a pearl on the table and do not say why. The hill folk bring nothing, and say a name once, quietly._
- **Risk:** it invites a favour system. The people you turn down looks like it lost, and the party look like hired hands.

### Option 3: the creature chooses

Anyone who has ridden a behemoth can hear the others, and the party are the people who hear them.

- **The pick:** two calls heard in the quiet week.
- **Recruits:** people on the way who touched the creature.
- **Objective:** what you heard, chosen from two.
- _In the quiet week, everyone who rode the Mossback dreams of heat. Some dream of salt._
- **Risk:** it makes the party chosen ones, and the narrator would have to state that the calls are real magic.

## 3. Words

**Instead of "the Road":** give the path no name at all. The journey is **the going**, and the mark it leaves on the map is **the trail** (v3's green trace). The old law moves from a path to the creature: _By old law a walker may go where it likes, and no one fences its going._ That is an open world written into law.

**Other choices:**

- **The drove** is good if the party are drovers, but it sounds like livestock.
- **The passage** suits sea and sky but sounds official.
- **The wake** suits water and sky, but it clashes with the Waking.

**The destination, per premise:**

| Premise | The going   | Where it ends      |
| ------- | ----------- | ------------------ |
| A       | the seeking | the sleeping place |
| B       | the carry   | the setting-down   |
| C       | the round   | the hearth         |
| D       | the going   | the casting ground |

**Under D, each kind's ground has a plain name:** the fold (moss), the cinder (serpent), the still (sky), the shoal (reef), the white (frost), the clearing (wood).

**Other words to change:**

- The Roadbook becomes the Trailbook, or the Gatebook under option 1.
- The Caravan's rule becomes "never sells the way back".
- "Walker" fits only creatures on legs. The narrator names the kind ("the serpent"), and each people keeps its own word.

## 4. Recommendation

**Premise D (the casting) with option 1 (the Open Gate).** From option 2 it takes the tokens: word of a pen arrives as a people's token, which hints at the creature and keeps the pick veiled.

**Why:** D is the only premise in which the creature both becomes part of the world and goes on living. It works for every kind, gives the garden a payoff, and gives the Wheelhouse a motive players can respect. The Gate explains the freeing (the design's "freed from captivity"), the pick, recruits and vows in one idea.

**What it keeps from v3:**

- The narrator rule, re-aimed: the coat is a fact, and what it keeps is not.
- Most of the evidence table: the hill folk's sleeper, the river folk's empty coat (now nearly literal, and still disputed), the Mothfolk's dreams, and the Wheelhouse dig that found a badger's sett.
- The garden, traces, waymarks, the Threshold, the calendar and Homing, the Glass Waste, the sealed-name feud (now about breaking a coat open), and the four endings with their peril-tag variants.

**What it changes:**

- The words: road, Roadbook, "road back".
- The Greenfold becomes the Mossback's casting ground, one of many.
- The safe ending casts instead of lying down.
- The setting-out beat belongs to the Gate.
- The sanctuary becomes a chart of coats plus echoes of the young, instead of enclosures (progression §3).
- The date "9 of 40" gains marks for each leg once journeys have more than one.

## 5. Questions for Misa

1. **Does the creature stay, or go on?** It stays in A, goes on in B and C, and does both in D. _My pick: D: the coat stays, and something young goes on._
2. **Who sends the party?** _My pick: the Open Gate, with the peoples' tokens as the two veiled cards._
3. **The sanctuary as a chart of the land** (coats, with echoes of the young), not painted enclosures? _My pick: yes._
4. **When do journeys with several legs arrive?** _My pick: not before the second tier. The Mossback has one leg; the first two-leg kind is the frost beast or the reef creature._
