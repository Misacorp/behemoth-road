## Emily Short (read in full)

- "Beyond Branching: Quality-Based, Salience-Based, and Waypoint Narrative Structures" (12 Apr 2016), emshort.blog. QBN is "structured around storylets unlocked by qualities" (numerical variables). Salience-based narrative "pick[s] a bit of content out of a large pool". QBN content "tends to be uninteresting until there are a fair number of storylets", yet large pools overwhelm; for salience systems build "a rudimentary set of content with sensible, broad defaults" first. Pitfalls: tracking progression by hand, losing the chain of causality; salience can fire at a dramatically wrong moment.
- "Storylets: You Want Them" (29 Nov 2019): storylets are "simple, atomic, robust, and recombinable"; content + prerequisites + effects. No pool-size numbers. "Time caves" (route-dependent content) are the most annoying to build; embed small ones inside a larger structure.
- For the game: storylets with tags, prerequisites, effects; salience pick for the daily card; quality gates for rarer beats; broad default cards per terrain and creature state first, then specific gated ones; log which qualities fired each card; short recombinable chains; playtest repetition instead of guessing pool size.

## Wildermyth (Worldwalker)

GDC 2022 IGS talk (members-only) names pitfalls: breach of trust, characters acting out of character, rage quits. Secondary (Steam forum snippet): events hand-written, lines varied per personality; fully procedural storylines can't keep tone. For the game: recombine hand-written cards; vary lines by role; keep characters consistent; avoid unexplained losses.

## Banner Saga (Stoic)

GameVerse 2014 interview with Drew McGee: scarce items fit a "dire world"; a hero's death should "pave the way for greater character development"; players should "worry about whether you could've made a better decision". For the game: shared resources as stakes; losses feed story; choices with no clearly right answer give friends something to discuss.

## Citizen Sleeper (How to Make an RPG case study)

"Clocks are circular progress bars that can be filled up a segment at a time" — "exposing the variables to the player". Dice as a daily action currency tied to condition; "played a day at a time". For the game: visible segmented clocks for the journey, threats and omens; hint at what a clock holds; avoid hidden hard failures.
