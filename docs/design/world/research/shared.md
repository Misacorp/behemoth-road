# Research: shared narrative in async co-op (2026-11-23)

Evidence caveat from the researcher: only two searches (Fallen London, Pandemic Legacy), search summaries only. Most points are the researcher's own knowledge and unconfirmed unless marked.

Sources (secondary): Fallen London Engadget interview 2017 (a continuing city, multi-step consistency QA); Failbetter writer guidelines (short promo blurb per piece; linked events can break the game; plan for players who back out); LSBU paper (forums read events as allegory); legacy games on Wikipedia and Kotaku (stickers persist, cards torn up, sealed packets open under conditions; Rob Daviau, Risk Legacy 2011).

## Principles

1. Story as a shared ledger, not a feed: past choices visible and permanent (strongest lever for a small group).
2. Conditional reveals: sealed packets open on a trigger, not a date; suits async play.
3. Small authored units written to be quoted; chains break when players skip them.
4. Asymmetric information produces talk (one person has the clue).
5. Digest, don't stream: about one scheduled post per day in a channel; no per-action pings.
6. Carry-over narrative and sideways: scars and facts, little raw power.

## Patterns ranked for this game

1. One dawn digest per day in the channel, posted by the tick: what changed on the map, 1–2 who-did-what lines, no spoilers; details in the app.
2. A role's private fragment (e.g. the Keeper): a sealed-card icon on their character; the channel says "the Keeper learned something", no content; they choose what to share.
3. Trigger-based events on map state (reaching a tile, a site found), not fixed dates; whoever arrives gets it, so nobody "missed it".
4. Credited moments: "Misa's Scout found the ford"; credit goes to whoever triggered it.
5. Archive as the campaign book: every card and fragment stored with who found it and when; an auto-written chronicle at season end; the next season opens with a 2–3 line "previously".
6. Scars and stickers on the behemoth and the map: persistent marks (a broken horn, a named landmark) as tags; cosmetic by default.
7. Sealed envelopes between seasons: 3–5 archive entries locked until a later-season condition ("reach the same valley again"), shown as a sealed slot (hint, not explanation).
8. Role-split clues: halves of a riddle to different roles, matched in chat; at most one a week, never gating progress.

## Carry-over limits

Carry knowledge, names, marks and cosmetics freely; at most a small capped effect; never stockpiles or stat growth.

## Pitfalls

- Spoilers in the channel: teasers only; content behind a tap.
- Missed beats: keep everything in the archive; a "since you last played" view.
- One very active player triggers everything: cap beats per day (1–2) and queue the rest.
- Phone text overload: 40–60 words per card, one image, one narrator voice.
- Hidden-info pressure: a role holding a secret must never gate progress; fallback clue after N days.
- Chain breakage: each beat standalone, the next optional.
