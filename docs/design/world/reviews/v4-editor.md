# Editor's review: world bible v4

## 1. Does v4 deliver the foundation?

**Mostly.** Creatures stay creatures, every kind pairs havoc with boon, and the far silhouettes make the world feel full. The law ("Where a creature walks, no gate stays shut") is the best new line.

**Gaps:**

- **Personality is listed, not shown.** The kinds have adjective lists, and two of them sulk. The Keeper owns the head, gaze and posture, so personality needs the channels that are free (sound, tracks, the garden), yet no kind has a sound or a habit. Only the Mossback ("starts eating before it has stopped walking") feels like someone.
- **Not yet beings everyone knows.** No individual creature has a name or a past except the Bellmother at Bellwater. Let each people name the same creature differently (research idea 7).
- **"Ancient" covers only the old kinds.** One line fixes it: the Mossback's moss is older than the village.
- **Every season starts at a pen.** The setting-out lines, "two pens" and the ledger put every creature in Wheelhouse hands. That makes the Wheelhouse all-powerful and strains fact 2 ("cannot be turned from it for long").
- **Stances.** The Wheelhouse is earned, but its real cost (fragment 7: a penned young creature lets a place die) belongs in its "Why" row. The Tally's grief is earned and then undercut by three things: the "cursed to wander" reading, a plan to buy a name that would "call every creature at once" (which makes them apocalyptic), and havoc examples that never show the claimed zero-sum ("another gives way"). Better: they want the name in order to burn it.

## 2. Originality and clichés

- **Orun** is Yoruba for heaven (Ọ̀run), a living faith term, so it must be replaced. **Larun** is close to Laran (the Etruscan war god), **Ith** is Íth (Irish legend), and **Sill** is an English word.
- **The misty headland with companions going on** is the Grey Havens, or Avalon. Use a concrete, odd image instead, such as the shingle where footprints stop.
- **Thornstag** echoes Mononoke's Forest Spirit and Cernunnos. **Cloudwhale**, **Reedwyrm** and **Cinder Serpent** are stock compounds.
- **Collisions.** The Tally, Hookwife Tallow and "A Caravan tally" (fragment 8) clash. "Bell" is everywhere (Bellmother, Bellwater, Bell Night, gate-bells, the Wheelhouse bell), and the hill-folk sign loses its weight.
- **Chosen ones.** Section 1 hedges well, but the calendar asserts it ("the Gate hears its calls"), and the pick makes the calls true, because the veiled creatures _are_ the calls. Let word of two pens set the pick, with members claiming a call afterwards and others doubting it. Recruits should join without a call.

## 4. Prose

### Eight weakest lines, with rewrites

1. "millers and bakers, not torturers." This is defensive and modern. **Rewrite:** "millers and bakers. They name every creature in the pens and know what each one eats."
2. "'know their place' is herders' talk." The idiom is about class. **Rewrite:** "creatures follow grass like any herd; 'a place waiting' is herders' talk for a good valley."
3. "cursed to wander until they break something." **Rewrite:** "every creature is a debt walking, and somebody always pays."
4. "taken root… a pinch of white sand." Sand cannot take root. **Rewrite:** "…a fern from the wood, and white dust caught between them."
5. "say Kelith's name twice, the way they say their own." **Rewrite:** "They tie a bell into the moss with its clapper free, ask Kelith's name, and say it back."
6. "each a little more weathered than the last. The newest has no moss on it yet." **Rewrite:** "Standing stones run off across the slope, one for every creature that came this way. The last has no moss yet."
7. "There was a place there waiting for a creature, and the Brightsmiths kept it in a collar until the place gave up." **Rewrite:** "A place out there waited for its creature. The Brightsmiths kept the creature collared, and the place stopped waiting."
8. "waiting for a garden to graze." **Rewrite:** "waiting for something to graze it green."

### Five strongest

1. "Pen four. Grazer, grown, origin unknown. Hay: two loads a day. Asks for nothing else."
2. "Bellwater, eleven houses. The bird-mother walked through them in the night and never knew they were there."
3. "Nobody looks up for long; everyone looks up."
4. "It walks into the Greyfold and starts eating before it has stopped walking."
5. "Every bloom is somebody's flood."

### Name sounds

The Wheelhouse and Tally names are excellent. Two gaps:

- **Kelith** and **Wrena** fit no people's rule.
- **Haldo** speaks in the season-one setting-out line, but section 8.3 allows eight names and Haldo is not one of them.

## 5. Sensitivity

- **The Tally** are mostly handled well: they come from every people, never strike a rider, and get a grieving ending. Three risks remain: the apocalyptic name plot, the "cursed" reading, and "Old Thirty" reading as a kill count. Keep the tone closer to grief than to whaling. The knotted cords echo khipu only faintly; avoid Andean trappings.
- **Glass Caravan:** "for a price" still comes before the code. Lead with the code.
- **Reedchoir:** "Lost children" whom they "sing to keep" still leans siren. A stake over the Reedwyrm would help.

## 6. Top six changes, ranked

1. **Fix names.** Replace Orun, Larun and Ith, rename the Caravan "tally", and thin out "bell". Give Kelith and Wrena a people, and add Haldo to season one's list or cut him.
2. **Keep the calls unconfirmed in practice.** Fix the calendar line, separate the pick from the calls, and let recruits join without one.
3. **Make the creatures personable through free channels.** Give each kind one sound and one habit, give each people its own name for a creature, and add the line that makes young ones ancient too.
4. **Re-earn the Tally.** Make their reading a debt rather than a curse, have them want the name in order to burn it, and put the Glass Waste cost in the Wheelhouse's "Why" row.
5. **Start some seasons outside a pen.**
6. **Sharpen the generic kinds** (Cinder Serpent, Reedwyrm, Thornstag), and move the way out off the misty headland.
