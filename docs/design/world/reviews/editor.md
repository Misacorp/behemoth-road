# Editor's review: world bible v2

## 1. What is alive, and what is borrowed

**Alive and original.** The restraint in "What the narrator states" is the best decision in the document. The material that works best is everyday and a little sly. The Wheelhouse creed ("bread is bread"). The Caravan selling river pearls back to the river folk, with both sides thinking it fair. The surveyor who dug Coldhollow and found a badger's sett. The Caravan tally that quietly undercuts the "Brightsmiths made the Waste" story. Coins that go from collar to hill. The year counted in walks, with a "long ten" that is no longer than the others: that is how real folk calendars behave. Garlands, traces and the misremembered "previously" legend turn mechanics into memory.

**Generic or derivative:**

- **"A white walker cut into a slope"** (1, 4.5). "White Walker" belongs to _Game of Thrones_, and every reader will hear it. This is the most urgent fix.
- **"It is not dead. Do not wake it."** echoes Lovecraft's "That is not dead which can eternal lie" closely enough to be noticed.
- **The horn at Tobbin's Rest** is the Kyffhäuser motif with its best-known British prop (Sewingshields, Alderley Edge). The motif is fine; the horn is a copy.
- **Hill-folk names** (Tobbo, Dilly, Tobbin's Rest, Marrow), together with a mown green road, read as the Shire.
- **The Kindled One**, a fire god with an altar and blue flames, is the stock element in the set. Its lines are good, but the frame is not.
- **The Reedchoir** are plain sirens so far.

## 2. Tone and prose

### Ten weakest lines, rewritten

1. "Singing, from somewhere in the reeds. It is very beautiful." This tells the reader what to feel. **Rewrite:** "Singing, somewhere in the reeds. The Mossback has stopped chewing."
2. "The chain lies slack. Someone back there is going to be shouted at." The joke is in a modern register. **Rewrite:** "The chain lies slack in the grass. Far behind, one smoke goes out."
3. "Leave a pearl for the one who sleeps downstream. It is not dead. Do not wake it." This is the Lovecraft echo. **Rewrite:** "Leave a pearl for the one downstream. Set it down; do not drop it."
4. "Four stones, the top one flat. Wren Long-step set it here last year, and someone has added a fifth since." The count is wrong: four stones cannot include a fifth. **Rewrite:** "Five stones, the top one flat. Wren Long-step set four of them last year. Nobody knows whose the fifth is."
5. "Rain comes over the grass in a grey wall." A grey wall of rain is a stock image. **Rewrite:** "Rain walks over the grass towards you. Behind it a dog is barking, then not."
6. "A hill is full of dreams, and the mist on it is them leaking out." "Leaking out" is too modern. **Rewrite:** "Every hill is full of the dead's dreams. The morning mist is the few that slip out."
7. "walkers are walking luck, and that luck dies far from home." The word "walk" is repeated. **Rewrite:** "a walker is luck with somewhere to be, and luck spoils far from home."
8. "herders tell stories to keep land." This is unclear. **Rewrite:** "herders tell stories so nobody ploughs their hills."
9. "An old walker forgets the way home, and a bell it can hear reminds it." This is clumsy. **Rewrite:** "Old walkers forget the way home. We made them something to listen for."
10. "Rain moving east across the window." (digest 2) "Window" is interface jargon in text meant to be in the world. **Rewrite:** "Rain moving east across the Tallgrass."

### Five strongest lines

1. "We made the collars to ring if they strayed. They never strayed. They only stopped."
2. "Dug the hill at Coldhollow to the bedrock. Clay, stone, a badger's sett. No beast."
3. "Bread doesn't ask what turned the wheel."
4. "One has gone out, and a girl wades in after it."
5. "Far behind you, the Wheelhouse turns a little faster, and the bakers light their ovens early."

### Sound of the names

The Wheelhouse names (Master Cogan, Hookwife Tallow, Second Wright) and the Brightsmith hammer marks are excellent and fully consistent. Elsewhere the names drift from the table's own rules:

- **Hill folk:** "Dilly" and "Tobbin" break the -o/-ow/-en endings. "Hessen" is a real German state.
- **River folk:** "Ilvi" breaks the -ai/-un endings. "Ulle" is a near-match for Ullr, a Norse god, which the research cautions against.
- **Glass Caravan:** "Brisk" is an English word, and "Dasko" ends in -o, the hill folk's ending.
- **Reedchoir:** "Aiwe" has no doubled vowel.
- **Overlap:** Hessen (hill folk) sounds like Thess and Hesh (Mothfolk), so the two peoples blur together.

### Register

There is no faux-archaism, which is right. The slips all go the other way, towards modern speech: "window", "shouted at", "leaking out" and "very beautiful".

## 3. Myth quality

**Do the legends contradict?** Partly: most sit side by side rather than collide. The one real collision is buried: the hill folk keep a way to wake the sleepers, and the river folk believe that waking would end the rivers. That feud should be stated in the data so a sealed entry can surface it. The Mothfolk version is added on rather than opposed. Give them a stake as well: if a hill wakes, the dreams of the dead spill out.

**Explain the same evidence.** Each legend should account for the same observed facts (the three turns, the grass, the mist, the Waste). For example:

- **The three turns.** The hill folk say it is saying goodnight. The river folk say it is finding downstream. The Wheelhouse says any dog does it.
- **The Glass Waste.** The hill folk blame the Brightsmiths. The Caravan tally says the ground was always white.

**Mystery leaks.** The narrator rule says only "lies down; grass grows thick", yet the endings say "the hill it becomes" and "there is a new hill". If a hill visibly rises, the Wheelhouse's "hills are hills; walkers die" is simply wrong, and the mystery collapses into one faction being foolish. The fix is to let the narrator state that a hill comes, and move the argument to what is inside it: a sleeper, a grave, dreams, or (as the surveyor found) nothing at all. Then even the Wheelhouse has no bones to show, and no side holds the answer.

**Promise not kept.** Section 1 promises a "lovers" Brightsmith fragment, and none exists. Suggested fragment: _Carved inside a collar:_ "So you will hear me when you are far away."

**Kindled One.** Calling it a "deity seeking control" confirms a god in a world built on doubt. Keep it unnamed and unexplained: the fires lean, and nobody agrees why.

## 4. Cultural sensitivity

- **Lamp-down.** Floating lamps on water strongly evoke tōrō nagashi (Obon) and Loy Krathong, both living rites. Making it a rite for births creates some distance. A distinct object would create more, for example a reed boat carrying a single pearl, or seed husks.
- **The Glass Caravan.** Desert nomads who sell any story and resell offerings edge towards the "untrustworthy travelling trader" stereotype, which has Orientalist and anti-Romani echoes. Give them a code they never break: they never sell the road back, never lie about water, and keep a debt for a hundred years.
- **Reedchoir names.** Names made only of vowels (Ooa) for a singing swamp people can read as a caricature of Pacific or Indigenous languages. Build their names from reed sounds instead (sh, wh, soft s), still sung.
- **Handled well:** no Sámi terms, Uffington abstracted, the shapeless first walker, a Wheelhouse not cruel for flavour, "Behemoth" kept to the title.

## 5. Top eight changes, ranked

1. **Rename the hill figure.** "The chalk walker" removes the _Game of Thrones_ collision.
2. **Fix the narrator rule.** The narrator states that a hill comes; the legends dispute what is inside. Rewrite the Wheelhouse line as "A walker lies down to die like any beast, and grass covers it as it covers any grave."
3. **Make the legends explain the same evidence**, and state the hill-versus-river feud over waking as data.
4. **Replace the horn at Tobbin's Rest.** Instead, the village keeps the true name of one hill, sealed, and says it never; this ties into the existing taboo on names.
5. **Rewrite the Lovecraft fragment and add the missing "lovers" fragment.**
6. **Tidy the names.** Replace Ulle and Hessen, apply the table's sound rules strictly, and move the hill folk away from Shire names (Dilly, Tobbo).
7. **Remove modern register from text meant to be in the world.** Take out "window" from the digests, the "shouted at" line and "very beautiful", and fix the cairn count.
8. **Give the Caravan a code and the Kindled One no name.** One sentence of data each lifts them out of stock types.
